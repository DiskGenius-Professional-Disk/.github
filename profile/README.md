## Four Beats

1. **Name the disk.** Give the DiskGenius Professional session a short title before you open a partition map on a disk you own.
2. **Pin the live volumes.** Keep current volume layout and the disk recovery view on the DiskGenius Professional board and leave unused drives aside.
3. **Check the preview.** Read the DiskGenius Professional summary of the sector preview before you treat a layout as final.
4. **Close the loop.** Note which maps are ready so the next DiskGenius Professional visit does not start by hunting the partition map.

**Does DiskGenius Professional replace the paper partition sketch?**  
No. DiskGenius Professional sits beside the labels, service notes, and disks you already own on your own PC.

**Can two technicians share one computer?**  
Yes. Each person keeps a separate DiskGenius Professional profile.

**Will DiskGenius Professional stay workable on an older office laptop?**  
Yes. DiskGenius Professional is meant to stay light during a normal map session.

## Field Note

DiskGenius Professional is easier to trust when the map stays smaller than the disk it came from. A named session shows which owned disk is active, the pins show which partition map you will read today, and the closing note tells the next person which disk recovery view drives the volume layout. That record is short on purpose: not a copy of every sector, only the path back to the sector preview that is still open on a disk the reader owns.

The same loop fits a student lab disk you own, a client volume you already hold, or a computer shared by two technicians. Open the last DiskGenius Professional session, confirm one partition, and leave the volume layout easier to follow than you found it. Other programs stay where they are. DiskGenius Professional only holds the partition map, the sector preview, and the handoff.

## One Real Pass

Pick a disk that already has a name and that you own: a spare volume on your desk, a lab drive you hold, or a short test disk on this PC. Open it in DiskGenius Professional, keep only that map on the board, and read one sector preview you can point to. A pass is done when that preview stays tied to the volume layout and the unused drives are no longer in the way. DiskGenius Professional does not need a full rebuild of the partition map before it is useful.

If the pass feels crowded, the map is too wide. Drop anything that belongs to a different disk and leave it out of this workspace. The next pass can have its own title. What matters is that this one stays readable from the first partition to the last volume, with the summary still matching the disk recovery view on a disk you own.
